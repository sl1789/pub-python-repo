# Bates (2000) — *Post-'87 Crash Fears in the S&P 500 Futures Option Market*

- **PDF:** `1-s2.0-S0304407699000214-main.pdf`
- **Venue:** *Journal of Econometrics*, Vol. 94, pp. 181–238.

## Methodology (Monte Carlo variant used)
Comparative estimation of two nested risk-neutral option-pricing models
fitted to S&P 500 futures option prices over 1988–1993:

1. **Stochastic-volatility (SV)** model — square-root variance process
   correlated with returns (Heston / Bates 1996 family).
2. **Stochastic-volatility with jump-diffusion (SVJD)** — adds a Poisson
   jump component to the return process to explicitly model crash fears
   (Merton 1976 lineage).

Both models admit (semi-)closed-form prices via the **inverse Fourier
transform of the characteristic function**, so the bulk of the work is
**numerical integration**, not Monte Carlo. Monte Carlo enters only as a
diagnostic — sampling implied terminal distributions to compare them
with realised post-1987 S&P 500 returns. Estimation is via two-stage
nonlinear least squares on observed implied volatilities.

## Type of data
**Empirical.** S&P 500 futures option prices from the CBOE for the
period **1988–1993**. The data sample is deliberately post-crash to
isolate the change in the risk-neutral distribution induced by 19 Oct
1987.

## Computational setup
**Single machine.** Fourier-inversion pricing plus non-linear LS is
fully tractable on the workstations of the era. No parallel /
distributed / Spark.

## Key contribution
Documents that:

- The SV model alone requires *implausibly large* volatility-of-volatility
  and correlation parameters to match the observed negative skew in
  implied distributions.
- Adding jumps relaxes those parameters into plausible ranges, but the
  resulting SVJD model implies a frequency of large drops that **did not
  materialise** in 1988–93 — evidence of a persistent **peso problem** /
  crash premium.

## Strengths
- Joint examination of two leading alternatives within a *nested*
  framework, allowing apples-to-apples comparison.
- Long, clean sample of liquid index options.
- Clear separation of pricing performance, parameter plausibility, and
  ex-post consistency.

## Limitations
- Confined to S&P 500 futures options — generalisation to single-stock
  options not attempted.
- Both models assume a single jump intensity and a single SV process;
  modern empirical work prefers double-jump / multi-factor extensions.
- No backtest of hedging performance, only pricing.
- Estimation uses cross-sectional fits per day rather than full
  panel-MLE, which limits efficiency.
