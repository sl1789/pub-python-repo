# Jiang (2004) — *The Limiting Distributions of Eigenvalues of Sample Correlation Matrices*

- **PDF:** `25053330.pdf`
- **Venue:** *Sankhyā: The Indian Journal of Statistics*, Vol. 66, Part 1, pp. 35–48.

> **Relevance note:** A pure mathematical-statistics paper in random
> matrix theory. It is **not** about option pricing or Monte Carlo
> simulation of asset paths. It is relevant to the thesis only as
> background for any future extension that handles a *cross-section* of
> equities and needs to estimate (or test) the correlation structure
> when the number of assets $p$ is comparable to the sample size $n$ —
> e.g. multi-asset Monte Carlo or basket-option pricing.

## Methodology (no Monte Carlo)
Asymptotic random-matrix-theory analysis of the **sample correlation
matrix** $R_n$ generated from an $n \times p$ data matrix whose rows are
i.i.d. samples (and entries within a row are i.i.d. under the
hypothesis). Three results are proved:

1. The **largest eigenvalue** of $R_n$ converges almost surely to a
   constant when $n/p \to c \in (0, \infty)$.
2. Under one regime of $n/p$, the empirical spectral distribution
   converges weakly to the **Marčenko–Pastur law**.
3. Under a different regime, it converges to the **semi-circular law**.

The proofs use the Stieltjes-transform / method-of-moments machinery
standard in RMT.

## Type of data
**Synthetic / theoretical.** The paper proves limit theorems for
randomly generated matrices; no real dataset is analysed.

## Computational setup
**Pen-and-paper proofs.** No computational experiments, no parallelism,
no Spark.

## Key contribution
Extends the classical Marčenko–Pastur convergence result from the
sample *covariance* matrix to the sample *correlation* matrix, and
identifies a second regime in which the semi-circular law applies. This
is the right tool for testing the null hypothesis $R = I$ (uncorrelated
variates) in the high-dimensional regime $p \asymp n$.

## Strengths
- Mathematically rigorous, full proofs.
- Identifies two distinct asymptotic regimes — providing a complete
  picture under different growth rates of $n$ relative to $p$.
- Directly applicable to high-dimensional statistical tests where the
  classical chi-squared null distribution fails.

## Limitations
- No empirical or numerical illustration of how fast the convergence
  kicks in for moderate $n, p$.
- Independence of within-row entries is required for the general result;
  realistic data is rarely independent.
- No financial application worked out in the paper.
- The Tracy–Widom rate of convergence for the *normalised* largest
  eigenvalue is referenced but not derived for the correlation case.
