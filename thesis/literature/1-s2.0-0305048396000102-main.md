# Shanker, Hu, Hung (1996) — *Effect of Data Standardization on Neural Network Training*

- **PDF:** `1-s2.0-0305048396000102-main.pdf`
- **Venue:** *Omega — The International Journal of Management Science*, Vol. 24, No. 4, pp. 385–397.

> **Relevance note:** This paper is **not** about option pricing or
> Monte Carlo simulation in finance. It is a methodological study of how
> input transformations affect feedforward neural-network training. It
> appears in the literature folder presumably as supporting material for
> any later work that pre-processes empirical returns before feeding them
> into a learning-based pricing model.

## Methodology (no Monte Carlo variant)
Controlled empirical experiment comparing two data-standardisation
schemes for feedforward neural networks on **two-group classification**
problems:

1. **Linear transformation** to $[0,1]$ via the observed range.
2. **Statistical standardisation** $(x - \bar x)/\hat s$.

Networks are evaluated under three performance measures across multiple
factor levels: network architecture (number of hidden units), sample
size, group-1 proportion, and three classification difficulty levels
defined by the input distributions.

## Type of data
- **Synthetic** for the main factorial experiment (problems generated
  from chosen multivariate distributions).
- One **empirical** illustrative dataset from American Telephone and
  Telegraph Company.

## Computational setup
**Single machine** — the paper predates GPUs and large-scale parallel
training. Training time is reported as one of the dependent variables.
No mention of distributed or parallel computation.

## Key contribution
Empirical evidence that:

- Standardised inputs *generally* improve classification performance,
  but the gain shrinks as network size and sample size grow — neural
  networks exhibit a "self-scaling" capability.
- Standardisation tends to **slow down** training in terms of iterations
  and wall-clock time.

## Strengths
- Carefully designed multi-factor experiment with three orthogonal
  performance measures.
- Distinguishes accuracy gains from computational cost.
- One of the early systematic studies on pre-processing for neural
  networks, frequently cited in subsequent ML-finance literature.

## Limitations
- Restricted to **two-group classification**; the generalisation to
  regression (option pricing) is non-obvious.
- Networks are small and shallow by modern standards (no deep
  architectures, no dropout, no batch-norm).
- Empirical illustration uses a single dataset; not generalisable to
  financial time series specifically.
- Does **not** address fat tails, autocorrelation, or non-stationarity
  that dominate financial-return data — so direct transfer of conclusions
  to MC-based option pricing requires further work.
